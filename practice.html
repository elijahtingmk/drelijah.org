The Terms, Once

What These Acronyms Mean

  • PRisMA — the DOSH 2024 framework itself: the staged process Malaysian employers follow to assess and manage psychosocial risk
  • PRiMA — the intervention side of that framework, and the name of the Action Plan you file. The spelling really is different; it is not a typo
  • LEO26 — the Stage I screening instrument, deployed across your work units
  • EPC23 — the Stage II employer practice checklist, triggered where Stage I returns a High organisational result
  • RICoV — the cut-off values that decide whether each component reads Low, Medium, or High risk
  • PTP — Psychosocial Trained Person: the DOSH-certified role permitted to conduct the assessment and sign the report

PRisMA 2024 · OSHA 1994 (Amended 2022) · ISO 45003:2021

End-to-End under One Practitioner

Under the Occupational Safety and Health (Amendment) Act 2022, Malaysian employers carry a statutory Duty of Care to manage psychosocial risks. PRisMA 2024 is the DOSH framework for meeting those obligations. This practice covers all three statutory stages.

Stage I

Hazard Identification

Screening of individual and organisational psychosocial risk across work units. Risk classified against the RICoV cut-offs per LEO26 component.

Instrument: LEO26
Stage II

Risk Assessment

Employer practice checklist deployed where Stage I returns High organisational status. Gaps prioritised across the seven PRiMA intervention themes for the Action Plan.

Instrument: EPC23
Stage III

Risk Control & Training

Capability-building interventions addressing the Training and Education theme directly — EPC23 items 16 (awareness) and 20 (training adequacy) — and supporting other themes where the identified gap is capability-related. Claimable under HRD Corp for levy-paying employers.

Delivery: HRD Corp Accredited Trainer
What This Means in Practice Most providers cover one part of the cycle. Assessors run LEO26 and EPC23 but cannot deliver claimable training; trainers deliver workshops but cannot conduct statutory assessment. The two credentials sitting together under one practitioner — DOSH-certified PTP and HRD Corp Accredited Trainer — collapse that coordination burden into a single engagement, with continuous methodology from assessment findings to training design to re-evaluation.

The DOSH Framework

The Seven PRiMA Intervention Themes

Every Action Plan is built against these seven themes as set out in the DOSH framework itself — not a model of this practice's own.

  • Social Support — supervisor and peer relationships, welfare provisions, protection from workplace violence
  • Working Environment — physical conditions, ergonomics, exposure to harmful agents, workplace civility
  • Job Matching — fit between job demands and employee skills, role clarity, competence-based task assignment
  • Training and Education — psychosocial-risk and mental-health awareness, work-life balance, role-specific preparation
  • Transparency and Fairness — clear roles, complaint-handling procedures, job security, wage adequacy, voice in work organisation
  • Control — autonomy over pace, location, and decisions; resource adequacy; supervisory communication
  • Workload — predictable hours, reasonable deadlines, periodic review of time demands

Consistent with the framework's Job Demands–Control–Support basis and ISO 45003:2021 alignment, PRiMA interventions prioritise job design, control, and support before individual capability building. Stage III training complements that structural work — it does not replace it.

Scope & Boundary

What This Practice Does — and What It Does Not

Full statutory PRisMA cycle, under one practice. Stage I (LEO26) and Stage II (EPC23) statutory assessment is conducted by a DOSH-certified Psychosocial Trained Person — the named practitioner on your report. The Stage III controls that follow are designed and delivered by the same practitioner, as an HRD Corp Accredited Trainer, working directly from those findings — without the methodology drift that comes from running separate assessment and training providers. The 12-month statutory re-evaluation continues under the same PTP framework, preserving methodology continuity from cycle to cycle.

Controls are selected on assessment evidence, from the DOSH menu. Every Stage III intervention is drawn from the PRiMA Plan of Actions menu against the specific gaps your assessment identifies — not a fixed catalogue this practice happens to sell. Clients are free to source delivery of any recommended intervention elsewhere; the assessment and the controls are not contractually tied.

Training interventions, not clinical care. All Stage III programmes are organisational interventions. They do not constitute clinical assessment, psychological therapy, or medical treatment. Participants identified as needing clinical support are signposted to qualified providers.

Data handling under PDPA 2010. Employee psychosocial data collected under any phase of the cycle is processed under the Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (Section 5: Seven Personal Data Protection Principles). Data is held by ECS Training & Consultancy Sdn. Bhd. as data user. Cohort reporting is de-identified; individual results are not disclosed to leadership without explicit consent.
